Aug 2024 — Jun 2025 · IEEE UCLA

Micromouse

A PCB-based maze-solving robot, built for IEEE UCLA's Micromouse competition.

I designed and built a maze-solving robot from the ground up: laying out and soldering the PCB myself, then writing and tuning the embedded control running on an STM32. The board handles motor drive, sensor input, and power regulation in a small enough footprint to actually fit and move through the maze.

Beyond the hardware, most of the real work was in the navigation algorithms — getting the robot to explore an unknown maze, build a map as it goes, and then optimize its path once it's found the center. Debugging that loop, on real hardware with real sensor noise, taught me more about the gap between "works in simulation" and "works on the bench" than anything else I built that year.
